Below is what I pulled from several press releases and other sources following the progression of Scout.

Note: 1, 3 , 5 are of most imporance

1. Just-in-Time / Just-in-Sequence parts sequencing
   Trigger: A dedicated JIT facility that sequences parts for final assembly.
   Use case: Real-time orchestration from the plant’s build-order broadcast to supplier picking, sequencing, and line-side delivery. Vantiq can detect build-order changes, line stoppages, or missing or damaged parts, and re-sequence automatically. Sequencing errors stop the line, so latency and exception handling carry real money.
2. Supplier Park-to-plant logistics orchestration
   Trigger: The park is described as the central logistics hub feeding the main assembly operations, across three buildings plus the Production Center.
   Use case: Live visibility and coordination of intra-campus material flow: dock and yard scheduling, tuggers, AGVs, and conveyors, plus inventory buffers between buildings. Vantiq can provide an event-driven control tower that acts on delays, not just reports them.
3. Battery assembly operations
   Trigger: A building for high-volume battery assembly.
   Use case:
   Real-time quality and process monitoring on the line
   Thermal and safety event detection with automated response
   Predictive maintenance on assembly equipment
   Cell-to-pack-to-vehicle genealogy and traceability, which supports recalls and emerging battery-passport requirements
4. Accessories and upfitting
   Trigger: A building for upfitting and installing vehicle accessories.
   Use case: Order-driven customization. Vantiq can connect customer and dealer configurations to installation work orders, verify that the right accessories are installed on the right VIN, and push status back to customer-facing systems. Scout sells direct, so this matters to customer experience.
5. Multi-party supplier ecosystem integration
   Trigger: Several suppliers are co-located on-site, with contracts still being finalized, as part of a strategy for a fully integrated domestic supply chain.
   Use case: Each supplier brings its own MES and ERP systems. Vantiq can serve as the real-time integration and orchestration layer across companies, sharing events like schedule changes, quality holds, and shortages without a rip-and-replace. This is probably the strongest architectural story, and the timing is good because contracts and systems aren’t locked in yet.
6. Supply chain resilience and disruption response
   Trigger: The CEO talks about establishing a resilient supply chain ecosystem.
   Use case: Detect upstream disruptions such as late inbound shipments, supplier quality escapes, or weather, then model the impact on the build schedule and trigger mitigation workflows automatically.
7. Greenfield smart-factory and workforce enablement
   Trigger: A new facility, with about 1,000 new supplier jobs coming.
   Use cases:
   A digital twin and real-time operations baseline built in from day one
   Worker safety monitoring, such as forklift and pedestrian proximity and EHS events
   AI-assisted operator guidance and onboarding for a large, mostly new workforce
